# Display surfaces and presentation patterns for daily-content apps on Android (late 2026), applied to Definiday

Scope: what surfaces the best daily-content apps use, what Android 14-16 offers or restricts, presentation (store listing, onboarding, permissions), and the relevant Play policies. Researched 2026-11-06. "Verified" = read on the primary source page in this session. "Reported" = secondary source or search snippet only.

## Q1. Android surfaces: what is available, what is restricted (widgets, lock screen, QS tiles, notifications / Live Updates, lock-wallpaper replacement, AOD, Wear)

### Takeaway
The home-screen widget is the standard, Google-endorsed "glanceable daily content" surface, and Google now ships first-party guidance for it (Glance, generated previews, quality tiers, in-app pinning). Phone lock-screen widgets are back with Android 16 QPR2, but only in a "hub" mode used while charging or docked, so they do not replace the always-visible lock wallpaper. Live Updates explicitly forbid ambient or daily content. Replacing the lock wallpaper has no special Play restriction, but since Android 13/14 an app cannot read the user's current wallpaper to restore it later. The only clean "undo" is `clear(FLAG_LOCK)`, which falls back to the home wallpaper.

### Cited Findings
**Home-screen widgets (verified)**
- Google's Widget Quality guide defines 3 tiers: Tier 3 "Low Quality" (below the minimum bar), Tier 2 (the quality standard) and Tier 1 (exemplary, personalized "hero" widgets). One Tier 2 requirement is an accurate preview in the widget picker, set via `previewImage`/`previewLayout` or Generated Previews. — [Widget Quality Tiers](https://developer.android.com/design/ui/mobile/guides/widgets/widget_quality_guide?hl=en) (reported via search snippet)
- Generated widget previews (Android 15+) are dynamic and personalized and are "provided through a push API" (`GlanceAppWidget.providePreview` / `setWidgetPreview`). Keep `previewImage` as a fallback for older versions. They need compileSdk 35+ and are rate-limited (about 2 calls per hour by default, per localized pages). — [Generated previews (Glance)](https://developer.android.com/develop/ui/compose/glance/generated-previews.md.txt) (reported via search snippet)
- **Gratitude case study (Android Developers Blog, May 2026):** "a 25% increase in retention for widget users", "10% of total DAU have adopted widgets", "~1K weekly journal entries from widgets". The team moved from RemoteViews to Jetpack Glance and went from planning to shipping in under a month, saving ~50% of dev time. They shipped one widget per core user journey (prompts, affirmations, vision board, metrics), with dynamic color and adaptive resizing, and promoted widgets with in-app pinning (a bottom sheet plus `requestPinGlanceAppWidget`). Tip: keep the receiver class name and package unchanged when migrating, or existing widgets are deleted from home screens. Testing across OEM launchers was essential. — [Android Developers Blog: Gratitude widgets](https://android-developers.googleblog.com/2026/05/how-gratitude-widgets-boosted-user-retention-25-percent.html) (verified. The retention method, time window and comparison group are NOT disclosed, so this is a correlation among self-selected users, not proven causation.)

**Lock-screen widgets on phones (reported)**
- Google first said lock-screen widgets would come to phones after Android 16 QPR1. — [Android Central](https://www.androidcentral.com/apps-software/google-is-bringing-widgets-to-phone-lock-screens-with-android-16-qpr1)
- They actually shipped in Android 16 QPR2 (Beta 1, Aug 2025), behind a "Widgets on lock screen" toggle. On phones they are reached by putting the phone on a charger or dock ("hub mode"); tablets use a swipe. Google warns that "anyone can view them". Existing widgets generally work without changes. To launch activities without authentication, an app must declare `android:showWhenLocked="true"`. — [9to5Google](https://9to5google.com/2025/08/20/android-qpr2-lock-screen-widgets/); [Droid-Life](https://www.droid-life.com/2025/08/20/android-16-qpr2-adds-lock-screen-widgets-to-phones-again/)
- Samsung: before One UI 8, Samsung lock-screen widgets were first-party only. SamMobile *speculated* that One UI 8 (Android 16) could open them to third parties. One UI 8 does bring a "Now Bar" that Samsung says is "integrated with more third-party apps", without specifics. — [SamMobile](https://www.sammobile.com/news/one-ui-8-bring-third-party-widgets-lock-screen/); [9to5Google](https://9to5google.com/2025/07/09/samsung-one-ui-8-now-bar-third-party-apps/)

**Notifications and Live Updates (Android 16) (verified)**
- To qualify, a Live Update must use Standard, BigText, Call, Progress or Metric style, declare `POST_PROMOTED_NOTIFICATIONS`, request promotion (`setRequestPromotedOngoing`), be ongoing and have a contentTitle. It must NOT have a custom RemoteViews, be a group summary, be colorized, or sit on an IMPORTANCE_MIN channel. OEMs can add criteria. — [Live Updates doc](https://developer.android.com/develop/ui/views/notifications/live-update)
- Usage criteria: ongoing, user-initiated, time-sensitive. "Don't show ambient information, such as that about the user's environment, interests, or upcoming events, in a Live Update." Inappropriate uses: "Ads, promotions, chat messages, alerts, upcoming calendar events, and quick access to app features". For quick access, Google says to "use an app widget or a custom Quick Settings tile". "Posting unwanted Live Updates might cause users to revoke an app's posting permission." — [Live Updates doc](https://developer.android.com/develop/ui/views/notifications/live-update)
- Live Updates are bridged to some Wear OS devices. — same source

**Lock wallpaper replacement (verified on API reference)**
- `getDrawable(which)`, `peekDrawable`, `getFastDrawable` and `getWallpaperFile`: "Starting in Android 13, directly accessing the wallpaper is not possible anymore, instead the default system wallpaper is returned… From Android 14, this method should not be used and will always throw a SecurityException. Apps with MANAGE_EXTERNAL_STORAGE can still access the real wallpaper." Reading requires MANAGE_EXTERNAL_STORAGE or the system-only READ_WALLPAPER_INTERNAL. — [WallpaperManager reference](https://developer.android.com/reference/android/app/WallpaperManager)
- `clear(FLAG_LOCK)`: "clear the lockscreen wallpaper. The home screen wallpaper will become visible on the lock screen." `getWallpaperFile(FLAG_LOCK)` returns null if no lock-specific wallpaper is set. There are also `isWallpaperSupported()` and `isSetWallpaperAllowed()` (the latter can be false under device policy). — [WallpaperManager reference](https://developer.android.com/reference/android/app/WallpaperManager)
- A direct analog exists: "Bible Lock Screen" (F-Droid) "automatically places the daily Bible verse onto your favorite photo", which the user picks. It offers font, color, shadow, blur, size and vertical-position controls, refreshes "every morning", and declares SET_WALLPAPER, exact alarms, a foreground service, ignore-battery-optimizations and storage read. The page does not say whether it restores the original wallpaper. — [F-Droid: Bible Lock Screen](https://f-droid.org/en/packages/com.daklok.biblelockscreen/)
- Other daily lock-wallpaper apps exist (e.g. "Daily Affirmation Screen": "automatically change your wallpaper daily with a new affirmation"). Complaint data about these apps came only from AI-generated audit sites and is unreliable. — [AppGoblin: Daily Affirmation Screen](https://appgoblin.info/apps/com.davidschindler.dailyaffirmation); [AppGoblin: Every Day Spirit](https://appgoblin.info/apps/com.goodbarber.everydayspirit)

### Inferences
- Definiday's lock-wallpaper approach is a real, if niche, pattern (Bible Lock Screen, affirmation lock-screen apps). It is the only surface that is always visible on the phone's lock screen without hub mode, so it stays a defensible differentiator on Android, where always-on lock widgets are not available to third parties.
- The biggest user-perception risk is "you replaced my photo and I can't get it back". The app cannot back up the existing wallpaper (since Android 13/14, MANAGE_EXTERNAL_STORAGE is a Play-restricted permission, so don't use it). Two mitigations: (a) like Bible Lock Screen, let the user pick their own photo as the card background, with the word overlaid at the bottom; (b) offer a clear "Restore my lock screen" button that calls `clear(FLAG_LOCK)`, and say before activation that the home wallpaper will show on the lock screen afterwards. Also check `isSetWallpaperAllowed()` for managed devices.
- Live Updates are **not** suitable for "word of the day". Google lists ambient content and "interests" as disallowed. Keep a standard (BigText) notification at the user-chosen time.
- The home-screen widget is the largest missing surface. The Gratitude case is the most credible public evidence (Google-published, but correlational). A Glance widget (small 2x2 "word + gender/class", medium "word + definition", resizable, dynamic color option) plus an in-app "Add to home screen" via `requestPinAppWidget` is the Google-endorsed path. minSdk 26 supports `requestPinAppWidget` (API 26). This is from prior knowledge; that API page was not fetched in this session.
- Lock-screen hub widgets (Android 16 QPR2, charging/docked) come for free once a home widget exists. Low priority, but declare the keyguard category if needed. Not verified: the official doc URL tried returned 404.

### Gaps
- No official developer.android.com page on phone lock-screen (hub) widgets was retrieved (the URL tried 404'd). Exact manifest requirements (widgetCategory keyguard vs hub) are unverified.
- Quick Settings tiles (TileService, `requestAddTileService` on Android 13+), Always-On Display (no third-party AOD API on Pixel. Samsung AOD is customizable only via first-party/Good Lock) and Wear OS tiles/complications were not researched in this session. The only cited fact is that Google suggests QS tiles or widgets for "quick access", and that Live Updates bridge to Wear.
- Pixel "At a Glance" has no public third-party API, from prior knowledge. Not verified this session.
- Samsung One UI 8 third-party lock-screen widget support is unconfirmed (speculation only). Good Lock/LockStar was not researched.
- No reliable data was found on user sentiment (Play reviews) about apps that replace the lock wallpaper, or on battery impact.

## Q2. What successful daily-content apps use as their primary surface, and evidence that widgets drive retention

### Takeaway
Top quote and affirmation apps market home- and lock-screen widgets as a core feature, mostly on iOS, where the lock screen is open to widgets. Daily reminders/notifications are the other near-universal surface. The strongest Android evidence for widgets is Google's Gratitude case study (+25% retention among widget users, 10% DAU adoption). Most other "widgets double retention" claims are vendor blogs without data.

### Cited Findings
- Monkey Taps "Motivation – Daily quotes" listing stresses personalizing "your home screen" with quotes. One localized listing claims 32M+ users (unverified). — [App Store: Motivation](https://apps.apple.com/app/apple-store/id876080126) (reported via search snippet. The Google Play page could not be read.)
- A teardown of Monkey Taps' "Vocabulary – Learn words daily" (the closest competitor shape: word of the day plus widget) estimates ~$200K/month revenue and ~150K downloads/month, and calls widget integration a key differentiator. These are third-party estimates and unverified. — [ScreensDesign: Vocabulary](https://screensdesign.com/showcase/vocabulary-learn-words-daily)
- Competing quote/affirmation apps advertise Home Screen **and** Lock Screen widgets, StandBy and Apple Watch (iOS). — [Daily Affirmations: Today I am](https://apps.apple.com/app/id6770134405); [Motivation Daily Quote Widget](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/-/id1269495142); [Motivium](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/-/id6755540341)
- A Lazyweb study of 809 apps found home-screen widget upsells (prompts in onboarding/paywalls) rose from 3.4% of captured apps in 2023 to 7.5% in 2025 (55 of 730 companies). The authors call the slope "directional". — [Lazyweb research](https://www.lazyweb.com/research/is-the-home-screen-widget-upsell-growing)
- Gratitude: +25% retention for widget users, 10% DAU widget adoption, in-app pinning prompt. — [Android Developers Blog](https://android-developers.googleblog.com/2026/05/how-gratitude-widgets-boosted-user-retention-25-percent.html) (verified, correlational)
- Vendor claims that widgets "double retention" (Stormy AI) or reduce uninstalls (Sonar) come without data. — [Stormy AI](https://stormy.ai/blog/app-growth-cheat-code-widgets-haptics-retention); [Sonar](https://trysonar.app/blog/ios-widget-extensions-discovery-aso-impact) (claims, unsupported)

### Inferences
- For a word-of-the-day app, the industry-standard combination is: home widget (primary glance surface), a daily notification at a user-chosen time (habit trigger), and share-as-image (acquisition). Lock-wallpaper replacement is Definiday's Android-specific substitute for the iOS lock-screen widget that competitors rely on.
- Expect widget adoption around 10% of DAU (Gratitude benchmark), not a majority. The wallpaper and notification therefore remain the main surfaces for most users, and the widget upsell should sit in onboarding or at a contextual moment.

### Gaps
- No Android-specific surface breakdown was found for Motivation, "I am", Stoic, Bible verse apps or vocabulary apps (Play listings were unreadable via fetch). No public retention data from these companies.
- Finch and Duolingo mascot and widget strategies (e.g. Duolingo's streak widget) were not researched this session.

## Q3. Presentation: store listing, onboarding and permission priming, share cards, aesthetic, dark mode, Material 3, mascot, accessibility

### Takeaway
Google's own guidance for the Play listing: a short description of 80 characters or less that states the core benefit; 2-8 screenshots with the most compelling feature first and short captions; a required feature graphic (1024x500); no ranking or award claims; and localized screenshots when they contain text. For permissions, Google says to wait and ask in context, triggered by a user action, with a rationale screen when `shouldShowRequestPermissionRationale()` is true.

### Cited Findings
- Short description: convey the message in 80 characters or less and summarize the biggest benefits. It can appear outside the listing. Don't repeat it in the full description. — [Play Console Help: store listing best practices](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/13393723?hl=en) (reported via search snippet)
- Screenshots: 2-8 per listing. Lead with the most compelling feature and use short captions. Avoid repeating the same message across the short description, screenshots, feature graphic and video. No "#1", "App of the year" or award claims. Provide localized screenshots and videos per language when they contain text. The feature graphic is required to publish. — [Play Console Help 13393723](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/13393723?hl=en); [Play Console Help 9866151](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9866151?hl=en) (reported). Feature graphic size 1024x500 per a third party — [Gummicube](https://www.gummicube.com/blog/mastering-google-play-store-listing-requirements). Sources conflict on where the feature graphic shows: an agency says it only appears with a promo video, while Google says "various places".
- POST_NOTIFICATIONS (Android 13+): notifications are **off by default** for new installs. Apps targeting 13+ have "complete control over when the permission dialog is displayed". Google advises: "Before you ask users to grant any permissions, let them familiarize themselves with your app", trigger the prompt from a user action (e.g. tapping an "alert bell"), or wait "until the third or fourth time the user launches your app". "Request the permission in context… users are more likely to grant." Check `areNotificationsEnabled()` before posting. Users see how many notifications per day an app sends and can revoke the permission. — [Notification runtime permission](https://developer.android.com/develop/ui/views/notifications/notification-permission) (verified)
- Exact alarms: the user must be sent to a Settings screen (`ACTION_REQUEST_SCHEDULE_EXACT_ALARM`). Check the result in `onResume()`, listen for `ACTION_SCHEDULE_EXACT_ALARM_PERMISSION_STATE_CHANGED`, and "gracefully degrade" (e.g. `setWindow`) if denied. — [Android 14 exact alarm change](https://developer.android.com/about/versions/14/changes/schedule-exact-alarms) (verified)
- Glance makes dynamic color (Material You) and resizing easier, and helps widgets "match each user's home screen". The team aligned widgets with Material 3. — [Android Developers Blog: Gratitude](https://android-developers.googleblog.com/2026/05/how-gratitude-widgets-boosted-user-retention-25-percent.html) (verified)
- Customization is what a typography-on-photo lock-screen product offers: font, color, bold, shadow, transparency, blur, size and vertical position, plus the user's own photo. — [F-Droid: Bible Lock Screen](https://f-droid.org/en/packages/com.daklok.biblelockscreen/) (verified)

### Inferences
- Onboarding for Definiday: (1) pick a surface ("lock screen", "notification", "widget"), with a live preview of the card on a mock lock screen; (2) pick a time; (3) only then ask for POST_NOTIFICATIONS (contextual, user-initiated), and only if the notification surface was chosen. Never ask on first launch.
- Exact alarms: a word of the day does not need minute precision. A 10-minute `setWindow` or `setAndAllowWhileIdle` avoids the Settings detour entirely. Keep exact alarms, if at all, as an opt-in "precise time" toggle that degrades gracefully.
- Store listing: the first screenshot should show the lock screen with the word card (the unique surface), captioned in French. Use the short description (≤80 chars) for the benefit, e.g. "Un mot rare chaque jour sur votre écran de verrouillage" (54 chars). No "#1" claims.
- If a widget is added, offer a dynamic color option alongside Definiday's own palettes (both Material You users and brand-palette users are served).

### Gaps
- Material 3 Expressive guidance (2025) for widgets/apps, Finch/Duolingo mascot design practices, share-as-image card patterns, dark-mode-specific guidance and accessibility (contrast of text over a wallpaper photo, TalkBack on widgets, font scaling) were not researched with sources this session. Recommend a follow-up (m3.material.io, the Android accessibility docs).
- No Google data on notification opt-in rates with and without priming was found.

## Q4. Google Play policies: SET_WALLPAPER, FGS specialUse, SCHEDULE_EXACT_ALARM / USE_EXACT_ALARM, POST_NOTIFICATIONS, Play Protect and sideloading

### Takeaway
SCHEDULE_EXACT_ALARM is denied by default for new installs on Android 14+ (when targeting 13+). USE_EXACT_ALARM is reserved for alarm-clock and calendar apps, so Definiday does not qualify. Foreground service types must be declared in Play Console, and specialUse is a narrow exception that is reviewed. No wallpaper-specific Play restriction was found. Developer verification for sideloaded APKs starts 30 Sep 2026 in 4 countries, with a global rollout in 2027.

### Cited Findings
- SCHEDULE_EXACT_ALARM "is no longer being pre-granted to most newly installed apps targeting Android 13 and higher (will be set to denied by default)". Existing grants survive a device upgrade, but not backup/restore. Without it, `setExact()`, `setExactAndAllowWhileIdle()` and `setAlarmClock()` throw SecurityException (except `setExact` with an OnAlarmListener). Exemptions: platform-signed, privileged, power-allowlisted apps, and SYSTEM_WELLBEING role holders. — [Android 14: schedule exact alarms](https://developer.android.com/about/versions/14/changes/schedule-exact-alarms) (verified)
- USE_EXACT_ALARM is for "Calendar or alarm clock apps". "Apps will not be able to publish a version of their app with this permission in the manifest unless they qualify based on the policy language." — same source (verified). A third party reports Play rejections citing "Violation of Permissions policy" when exact alarm permissions are declared without the Play Console declaration. — [TechNetExperts](https://www.technetexperts.com/android-play-store-permissions/) (reported, secondary)
- Alternatives Google lists: WorkManager (15-min minimum with flex), `setAndAllowWhileIdle()`, `set()`, and `setWindow()` (minimum 10-minute window). — [Android 14: schedule exact alarms](https://developer.android.com/about/versions/14/changes/schedule-exact-alarms) (verified)
- Foreground services: apps targeting Android 14+ must declare each FGS type on the App content page in Play Console. TYPE_SPECIAL_USE may be declared only "in limited scenarios" when the use meets the other FGS requirements. All FGS types are subject to review. A policy update effective 26 Aug 2026 changes approved use cases (e.g. removes geofencing). — [Play Console Help: FGS requirements](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/13392821?hl=en); [Play Console Help 16965181](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/16965181?hl=en) (reported via search snippet)
- POST_NOTIFICATIONS is a runtime permission. Notifications are off by default on new installs (Android 13+). Apps don't need it to start an FGS, but the FGS notification is then shown only in Task Manager. — [Notification permission](https://developer.android.com/develop/ui/views/notifications/notification-permission) (verified)
- Live Updates need the non-runtime `POST_PROMOTED_NOTIFICATIONS`, and users can disable them per app. — [Live Updates](https://developer.android.com/develop/ui/views/notifications/live-update) (verified)
- Reading the current wallpaper requires MANAGE_EXTERNAL_STORAGE (Android 13+/14+). — [WallpaperManager](https://developer.android.com/reference/android/app/WallpaperManager) (verified). MANAGE_EXTERNAL_STORAGE is itself a Play-restricted "All files access" permission. This is from prior knowledge; the policy page was not fetched.
- Developer verification for sideloading: restrictions take effect **30 Sep 2026** in Brazil, Indonesia, Singapore and Thailand, with a global rollout in 2027 (no date yet). An "advanced sideloading" flow lets users still install unregistered apps with more friction. ADB installs are exempt (long-term status unconfirmed). Intermediate milestones conflict between sources. — [9to5Google](https://9to5google.com/2026/03/30/android-developer-verifier...); [Android Authority timeline](https://www.androidauthority.com/android-sideloading-changes-timeline-3679204/); [GSMArena](https://gsmarena.com/google_android_developer_verification_app_sideloading_changes_timeline-news-73354.php) (reported)

### Inferences
- Definiday should **not** declare USE_EXACT_ALARM (it would be rejected). If it keeps SCHEDULE_EXACT_ALARM, it needs a Play declaration/justification and a fallback path. Simplest compliant design: inexact `setWindow`/`setAndAllowWhileIdle` for the morning wallpaper and the timed notification, and drop exact alarms entirely.
- A specialUse foreground service just to redraw a wallpaper once a day is unlikely to pass review. Wallpaper rendering and setting fits within a BroadcastReceiver/`goAsync` or a short WorkManager job, with no FGS needed. Verify that Definiday has no FGS left.
- SET_WALLPAPER is a normal install-time permission, from prior knowledge. No Play policy section restricting it was found, but changing device settings without clear user consent could fall under the Device and Network Abuse/deceptive behavior policies. Get explicit opt-in and an easy revert (`clear(FLAG_LOCK)`). This is an inference, not verified policy text.
- Sideloaded distribution (APK outside Play): from 2027 globally, the developer identity must be verified, or users face the "advanced" flow. Plan to register in the Android Developer Console even if not on Play.

### Gaps
- The Play policy center text for exact alarms (answer/12253906#exact_alarm_preview) could not be read: the fetch returned the deadlines page.
- No Play policy text specific to SET_WALLPAPER or wallpaper-changing apps was found.
- Play Protect heuristics for sideloaded APKs (e.g. warnings for apps declaring SET_WALLPAPER + exact alarms + boot receiver) were not found. There is no public documentation of the flags.
- The latest confirmation that the 30 Sep 2026 verification date held was not found (the sources date from before Sep 2026).
