# Benchmark: English-language "word of the day" and vocabulary apps (as of 6 Nov 2026)

All store figures were pulled on **2026-11-06**, US storefront (Google Play `hl=en_US&gl=US`; App Store US page plus the iTunes Search/Lookup API). Ratings and prices change often, so treat them as a snapshot. "Unverified" marks third-party estimates or claims I could not confirm at a primary source.

Store URL patterns used below:
- Google Play: `https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=<package>`
- App Store: `https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id<trackId>`
- iTunes API: `https://itunes.apple.com/lookup?id=<trackId>&country=us`

## 1. Which apps lead the category, and how big are they?

### Takeaway
In the "learn rare and interesting words daily" niche, **Vocabulary by Monkey Taps** clearly leads, with 222K iOS ratings at 4.8 and 5M+ Android installs. Its nearest pure-play rivals are the **Word of the Day・Vocabulary** app by Vocabulous (iOS only, 39K ratings), **WordUp/Zann** (Geeks Ltd, a learner-oriented app) and **Atlas**. Dictionary brands such as Merriam-Webster and Dictionary.com have huge install bases, but in their apps the word of the day is a side feature. Elevate and Magoosh sit next to the category rather than in it.

## 2. Daily content format and delivery (widget, lock screen, notification, wallpaper)

### Takeaway
The standard daily card has five parts: word, part of speech, short definition, example sentence and audio pronunciation. The better apps add something extra: an **illustration** (Vocabulous, Atlas, Kawaii), **etymology/origin** (Dogan Ekici's widget: 4,000+ words with origins; "Uncommon Words" with origin notes), or a **short article** (Atlas). Delivery is now **widget-first**: home and lock-screen widgets plus timed notifications, and Apple Watch for some. I found **no competitor that writes the word onto the lock-screen wallpaper image itself**. All of them use widgets.

### Cited Findings
- **Vocabulary (Monkey Taps)**
  - Card content: definition, phonetics, example sentence and audio. The home feed is a full-screen card that you swipe up for a new word, "short-video style" — [woshipm analysis, 23 Sep 2025](https://www.woshipm.com/?p=6272227); [ScreensDesign](https://screensdesign.com/showcase/vocabulary-learn-words-daily).
  - The listing advertises a "word of the day widget and lock screen word updates", "More than 10,000 English words, organized by difficulty level and topic" and Wear OS support — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/vocabulary-learn-words-daily/id1084540807); [Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.hrd.vocabulary).
  - Apple's editorial recommends the Lock Screen widget and an Apple Watch face so "users see a new word whenever they check the time". Users can set how many daily reminders they get and pick a pronunciation voice — [Apple story](https://apps.apple.com/mv/iphone/story/id1675956696).
  - In onboarding, you set exact start and end times for notifications rather than a simple opt-in — [ScreensDesign](https://screensdesign.com/showcase/vocabulary-learn-words-daily).
  - Content is ranked using data: Monkey Taps uses feedback and interactions to surface its most-liked words, e.g. "mendacious", "seraphic", "meretricious", "reticent", "ameliorate" — [Apple story](https://apps.apple.com/mv/iphone/story/id1675956696).
- **Word of the Day・Vocabulary (Vocabulous)**: each daily word is "hand-selected by our editors" and comes with examples, audio and "Thoughtful illustration". It offers Home Screen and Lock Screen widgets and custom notifications — [iTunes lookup](https://itunes.apple.com/lookup?id=987136347&country=us).
- **Word of the Day: Widget (Dogan Ekici)**
  - One word a day with definition, pronunciation (with a slow replay), an example and synonyms. 9,111 words graded A1–C2; "over 4,000 words show where they come from".
  - "Not today's word for you? Get a new one, once a day."
  - Small and medium Home Screen widgets, Lock Screen widgets, and an "extra-large widget on iOS 27".
  - You can hear the word from the widget, from Control Center, or by asking Siri. The widget "changes after midnight" and matches the word in the app.
  - Extras: short real-speech video clips, links to a learner's dictionary, translation and news search. Users praise its "link to the oxford dictionary" — [iTunes lookup](https://itunes.apple.com/lookup?id=1536210624&country=us); [App Store reviews](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id1536210624).
- **Atlas**: the word of the day comes with "short, engaging articles using that word" and "a custom illustration", plus daily reminders and Home Screen widgets — [iTunes lookup](https://itunes.apple.com/lookup?id=1438344260&country=us).
- **Vocabulary Widget (Kawaii)**: the widget shows "a fresh word every hour" with part of speech and definition, and you can save (heart) or share from the widget. There is a daily hand-picked word of the day "paired with an original illustration… same word for everyone, so it's a great one to share". It has 1,200 words — [Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.kawaii.vocabulary).
- **Widget Vocabulary (Orbit Studios)**: "New words automatically appear throughout the day" on the Lock Screen. It is multi-language (Spanish, French, German…) and offers 1,000+ quiz levels — [iTunes lookup](https://itunes.apple.com/lookup?id=6775767149&country=us).
- **Merriam-Webster**: the word of the day sits inside a full dictionary. Pronunciations are "voiced by real English speakers, not text-to-speech robots". The Apple Watch extension shows the word of the day — [iTunes lookup](https://itunes.apple.com/lookup?id=399452287&country=us).
- **Dictionary.com**: word of the day, editorial articles on word origins, and "Dictionary Shuffle" (a random word) — [Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.dictionary).
- **Duolingo (widget reference case, 29 Aug 2023)**
  - The widget was designed to "prompt action", showing the streak and whether it is "at risk". Duo's expression grows more panicked as midnight approaches, and about 25 new illustrations were planned.
  - "Half of learners with the widget have a streak of at least six months." Widget users had "much better retention" even after controlling for commitment.
  - Installs rose sharply after an in-app animated install explainer shown after lessons. Awareness and installation friction were the main obstacles, and Android fragmentation was a significant engineering challenge — [Duolingo blog](https://blog.duolingo.com/widget-feature).

### Inferences
- Definiday puts content **on the wallpaper itself**, which seems unique among the apps reviewed. It sidesteps widget-install friction (Duolingo's main obstacle) and the widget refresh bugs users complain about (see section 6). It is worth marketing explicitly as "no widget to set up".
- Content cues worth considering, mapped to Definiday:
  - "same word for everyone → shareable" (Kawaii): matches Definiday's daily words.
  - "skip today's word once a day" (Dogan Ekici).
  - widget-side audio and actions (save/share).
  - dictionary links: users explicitly praise them, which supports Definiday's dictionary-links feature.
  - illustration per word (Vocabulous, Atlas, Kawaii): could map to a Monsieur Glose illustration.
- A mascot whose mood reacts to state, as with Duo's panicking face, has documented retention value. Monsieur Glose could carry a similar "quiz pending tonight" state.

### Gaps
- I did not verify whether any English app offers an **Android lock-screen wallpaper** mode (Android has no lock-screen widgets on most devices). None of the descriptions I read mention it.
- No source checked uses iOS Live Activities for a word of the day.
- No app was confirmed to mix public-domain quotes with words. Monkey Taps does quotes in its separate Motivation app.

## 3. Personalisation, learning mechanics, quizzes, streaks, lists

### Takeaway
Almost every leader opens with an **onboarding questionnaire** covering goals, level, topics and reminder times, and many add a placement test. Retention then comes from **short quizzes with spaced repetition, streaks, favourites and custom lists**. Vocabulary keeps the mechanics light (feed, collections, periodic quizzes, streak). Atlas, WordUp and Vocabulous go heavier (curricula, games, crosswords, AI tutors).

### Cited Findings
- **Vocabulary (Monkey Taps)**
  - Onboarding is long: about 20–22 steps per ScreensDesign; AppLlama lists 42 screens. It covers an age and goals quiz, notification time window, app icon choice, theme choice, learning goals ("Enhance my lexicon", "Improve my job prospects") and a cross-sell for a "Self-Growth Essentials bundle", then the paywall — [ScreensDesign](https://screensdesign.com/showcase/vocabulary-learn-words-daily); [AppLlama](https://appllama.io/apps/1084540807/vocabulary-learn-words-daily).
  - woshipm describes staged questions on age, goals, daily study time, interests, level and objectives, plus icon, theme and widget customisation, and judges the flow "somewhat long" — [woshipm](https://www.woshipm.com/?p=6272227).
  - Mechanics: save words to your own collections, "dislike" a word to train recommendations, periodic quizzes (the first after a few words) with right/wrong feedback only, and a streak banner. AppLlama counts screens in Topic Browsing, Vocabulary Library, Practice, Stats and Assessment, Widgets and Alarm — [ScreensDesign](https://screensdesign.com/showcase/vocabulary-learn-words-daily); [AppLlama](https://appllama.io/apps/1084540807/vocabulary-learn-words-daily).
- **Vocabulous Word of the Day**: a vocabulary assessment plus goal questions produce a "personalized learning plan". Other features:
  - 1-minute daily quizzes using spaced repetition.
  - "Stop Saying" collections, which suggest stronger alternatives to common words.
  - Games: Hangman, Gaps (a 5-letter guessing game), Word Relay, and themed crosswords across 15+ topics.
  - Streaks and daily challenges.
  
  [iTunes lookup](https://itunes.apple.com/lookup?id=987136347&country=us).
- **Atlas**: a proficiency test running from Beginner to "Super-Proficient". Other features:
  - Structured curriculum, module exams, and a personal "Vocabulary Journal" with custom lists.
  - Six exercise types: flashcards, gap-fill, definition matching, spelling, dialogue gap-fill, meaning from context.
  - An "Atlas AI Tutor" and spaced repetition.
  
  [iTunes lookup](https://itunes.apple.com/lookup?id=1438344260&country=us).
- **WordUp / Zann**: an "AI-powered Knowledge Map" over the "25,000 most common English words", 9 challenge types, spaced repetition, examples from movies, quotes and news, AI chat ("Lexi", "Fantasy Chat" with AI celebrities), a Chrome extension and offline mode — [App Store lookup](https://itunes.apple.com/lookup?id=1365078730&country=us); [Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=co.wordupapp.app).
- **WordPal**: synonym multiple-choice backed by spaced repetition, 5 mastery levels, a daily goal, reminders and streaks. It gauges your level first — [iTunes lookup](https://itunes.apple.com/lookup?id=1418579583&country=us); [App Store review](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id1418579583).
- **Word of the Day: Widget**: "Daily Review" brings words back "just before you'd forget them". Up to 3 new words a day are free; 5, 7 or 10 with Premium. Daily puzzles are the same for everyone and shareable: Daily Climb, Word Groups (Connections-like) and Word Chain. Streaks, plus "My Words" bookmarks — [iTunes lookup](https://itunes.apple.com/lookup?id=1536210624&country=us).
- **Magoosh**: 1,200 GRE words; beat levels to unlock harder ones; words you miss repeat through spaced repetition; an account is needed to sync — [Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.magoosh.gre.quiz.vocabulary).
- **Elevate**: 40+ games, personalised daily workouts of 3–5 games, adaptive difficulty, a workout calendar with streaks, and weekly reports — [iTunes lookup](https://itunes.apple.com/lookup?id=875063456&country=us).
- **Kawaii Vocabulary Widget** positions itself against gamification: "no flashcards, no streaks to keep up, no time set aside" — [Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.kawaii.vocabulary).
- **MyVocab**: favourites, collections, adding your own words, definition quizzes and review history — [iTunes lookup](https://itunes.apple.com/lookup?id=1614457796&country=us).

### Inferences
- Definiday's single evening quiz on the day's words is closest to Vocabulous' "1-minute daily quiz". That format seems well calibrated. A light spaced-repetition layer, re-quizzing words from 3 and 7 days earlier, is the most common upgrade among the leaders.
- "Same puzzle for everyone + share your score" (Wordle-style, used by Dogan Ekici and Vocabulous) is the main social hook in the category.
- Unlocking mascot looks through quizzes is a reward mechanic I did not see in any English competitor. It is a differentiator.

### Gaps
- I could not view the exact onboarding screens of Vocabulary: AppLlama blurs them behind its Pro tier.
- No published retention data for any of these apps except Duolingo's widget statements.

## 4. Customisation (themes, fonts, backgrounds) and social sharing

### Takeaway
Visual customisation is a core selling point for the aesthetic leaders. Vocabulary offers themes, app icon and voice and got an Apple Design Award finalist nod for visuals. MyVocab and Kawaii market themes and fonts explicitly. Dogan Ekici sells "your own photo behind the word" as Premium. Sharing is mostly a share button, plus "same word for everyone" designs.

### Cited Findings
- **Vocabulary**: theme and app-icon selection during onboarding; icon style, app theme, UI style and voice accent (American, British, Australian…); reminder frequency. The v7.7.0 release notes read "New themes and tons of new words!" — [ScreensDesign](https://screensdesign.com/showcase/vocabulary-learn-words-daily); [woshipm](https://www.woshipm.com/?p=6272227); [iTunes lookup](https://itunes.apple.com/lookup?id=1084540807&country=us).
- **Vocabulary**: 2025 ADA finalist in Visuals and Graphics — [Apple Newsroom](https://www.apple.com/newsroom/2025/06/apple-unveils-winners-and-finalists-of-the-2025-apple-design-awards/). A Fueled "App of the Week" piece called it attractive and well designed, making good use of native iOS features (search snippet only) — [Fueled](https://fueled.com/?p=30821).
- **Kawaii Vocabulary Widget**: "18 themes to style both the widget and the app" (Paper, Notebook, Chalkboard, Library, Typewriter, Midnight, Botanical…) with "Elegant serif type". The free tier has 6 word lists and 5 themes; Pro unlocks 40 lists and 18 themes. Lists include "Rare Words, Archaic Words, Eponyms, Loanwords… Beautiful Words" — [Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.kawaii.vocabulary).
- **MyVocab**: "Customize your experience with fonts, backgrounds, and themes. Share interesting words with friends." One user review says "I love the cool styles you can choose" — [iTunes lookup](https://itunes.apple.com/lookup?id=1614457796&country=us); [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id1614457796).
- **Word of the Day: Widget**: paper or photo themes, "some of them moving"; light and dark follow the system; Premium unlocks every photo theme and "your own photo behind the word, in the app and in the widgets" — [iTunes lookup](https://itunes.apple.com/lookup?id=1536210624&country=us).
- **Dictionary.com**: only a dark mode is advertised — [Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.dictionary).
- **Sharing**: Kawaii lets you share from the widget; Dogan Ekici shares daily puzzle scores; WordUp lists "social features" — [Google Play Kawaii](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.kawaii.vocabulary); [lookup](https://itunes.apple.com/lookup?id=1536210624,1365078730&country=us).

### Inferences
- Definiday's 18 palettes and 14 fonts are on par with or above the market (Kawaii has 18 themes; Vocabulary's exact theme count was not found). Rivals use themes as the **main paywall unlock**, which suggests customisation is perceived as valuable. Definiday giving all of it away free (no ads, no account) is a strong positioning message.
- A "share as image" card in story format (word plus palette plus font) is something I did not see documented. It is a gap I could not verify, and possibly an opportunity.

### Gaps
- The exact number of themes and fonts in Vocabulary was not found.
- Instagram-story share cards were not confirmed for any app.

## 5. Monetisation (models, prices, paywall placement)

### Takeaway
The pure-play leaders use **subscriptions with a free trial and a paywall right after onboarding**. Annual plans run about **$20–$60/year**, with lifetime tiers of $50–$150 and A/B-tested price ladders visible in their in-app purchase lists. Some are now **hard-paywalled** (MyVocab). Dictionary brands rely on **ads plus a cheap ad-removal**, and ads are their main source of complaints.

### Cited Findings
All prices are App Store US in-app purchase lists, pulled 2026-11-06.

- **Vocabulary (Monkey Taps)**
  - Listed prices: Premium (1 month) $14.99; Premium (1 year) $35.99 / $59.99; Lifetime $149.99; other "Vocabulary Premium" items at $9.99, $14.99, $29.99, $47.99, $59.99 and $69.99 (probably price tests or regional variants). Google Play range: $2.99–$169.99 per item — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id1084540807); [Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.hrd.vocabulary).

## 6. What users love and hate (review mining)

### Takeaway
Users **love** effortless exposure ("glance and learn"), the widget, clean aesthetics, choosing levels and categories, dictionary links, and no ads. They **hate**:
- word selection that is too easy for natives (very common);
- widgets that stop updating;
- words that change too often to learn;
- wrong pronunciations;
- intrusive ads and aggressive paywalls;
- paid content or premium tiers being removed.

### Cited Findings

## 7. What makes the #1 app (Vocabulary by Monkey Taps) successful

### Takeaway
Vocabulary's success comes from several things at once:
- an **ultra-simple core loop** (one word per full-screen card, swipe for more);
- **aesthetic polish** recognised by Apple (ADA 2025 finalist), with personalisation of theme, icon and voice;
- a **long, investment-building onboarding** that ends in a trial paywall;
- **ambient delivery** through lock-screen and home widgets, Apple Watch and timed notifications;
- a **studio playbook** reused across the Motivation, I am and Facts apps, with data-driven content ranking and cross-selling.

Its weaknesses are word difficulty, widget reliability and paywall pressure.

### Cited Findings
- Core format: a TikTok-like card feed with "variable reward" (a random new word on each swipe). The app targets a defined niche ("language enthusiasts who want rare, expressive words"). Personalisation and collections support autonomy and competence (Self-Determination Theory). The team is fully remote and applies the same approach across its portfolio — [woshipm, 23 Sep 2025](https://www.woshipm.com/?p=6272227).
- The marketing tagline promises "sound ludicrously articulate without trying too hard" and "Learn new words with minimal time investment, simply scrolling through your phone" — [iTunes lookup](https://itunes.apple.com/lookup?id=1084540807&country=us).
- Onboarding takes about 20+ steps: goals, notification window, icon, theme, a cross-sell, then a trial paywall with one annual plan — [ScreensDesign](https://screensdesign.com/showcase/vocabulary-learn-words-daily).
- Apple editorial featuring includes an App Store story with a founder quote: "By using data-driven insights, we can make the learning process more effective and engaging for our users" (Leopold Riola) — [Apple story](https://apps.apple.com/mv/iphone/story/id1675956696).
- Portfolio: Motivation (5M+ downloads in the last year), I am (6M), Vocabulary (3M), Facts and others, with an in-house content team ("writers… with data science and AI") — [monkeytaps.net](https://monkeytaps.net/).
- Longevity and iteration: released Feb 2016 and still updated weekly (v7.6.1 on Oct 27, 2026; v7.7.0 on Nov 4, 2026) — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/vocabulary-learn-words-daily/id1084540807); [iTunes lookup](https://itunes.apple.com/lookup?id=1084540807&country=us).
- The app is localised as an English-learning product in other markets (e.g. the Mexican App Store title "Vocabulary - Aprende inglés"), widening its audience beyond natives — [search result: App Store MX](https://apps.apple.com/MX/app/id1084540807).

### Inferences
- The pattern behind the leaders' wins: a **beautiful, single-purpose daily card** plus **ambient surfaces** (lock screen) plus **identity-flavoured onboarding** ("sound articulate"). Mechanics depth (spaced repetition, games) is secondary for the #1 app.
- Definiday already matches the core pattern: daily card on the lock screen, rich customisation, and a light daily quiz. It differs in three ways:
  - it delivers on the wallpaper rather than through a widget;
  - its content is rare and curated, the main weakness users cite in Vocabulary;
  - it has a mascot reward loop and no paywall.
- Ideas from the leaders that could fit Definiday's constraints (offline, no account, no ads):
  1. A short identity onboarding (goal plus palette/font choice plus reminder time) to build investment.
  2. A share-as-image card of the day's word.
  3. Optional spaced re-quiz of past words.
  4. A "skip/replace today's word" option.
  5. An in-app explainer for setting the wallpaper or lock screen (Duolingo found install education was the key lever).
  6. Mascot states tied to the evening quiz (Duo-style).

### Gaps
- Vocabulary's actual revenue and retention are not public; estimates conflict by an order of magnitude ($50K to $800K a month on the same ScreensDesign page).
- No founder interview about strategy was found beyond the single Apple quote. The We Are Founders article is paywalled.
